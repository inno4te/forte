📅 Week 1 · Lesson 1
Foundations of Artificial Intelligence
⏱️ ~35 minutes
📖 Read & Practice
🎯 Core Concepts

1.1 How Did We Get Here? The Story of the Web

To understand Artificial Intelligence, we first need to understand the digital world it lives in. The internet has gone through three major transformations — and we are living through the third one right now.

EraDescriptionKey Platforms
Web 1.0 (1990s–2000s)The 'Read-Only' web. Static HTML pages created by a few, consumed by many.Yahoo, AOL, GeoCities, Netscape
Web 2.0 (2004–2020)The 'Read-Write' web. Social media, blogs, YouTube. Anyone could create and share content.Facebook, YouTube, Twitter, WordPress
Web 3.0 (2020–Present)The 'Read-Write-Own' web. Powered by AI, blockchain, and decentralization. AI is the defining tool.ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Web3 apps

1.2 What Is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence means giving computers the ability to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence. A machine becomes 'intelligent' when it can do four things:

👁️ PERCEIVE
Gather and interpret information from the environment — text, images, sound, sensor data.
🧠 ANALYSE
Process and find patterns in that information at massive speed and scale.
🎯 DETERMINE
Make a decision based on analysis — choosing the best response or action.
⚡ ACT
Take the appropriate action — generate text, make a recommendation, complete a task.
💡 Local Context
Think about walking through the Marché Mokolo in Douala. You see stalls (perceive), notice the freshest tomatoes (analyse), decide to buy (determine), and walk over (act). AI replicates this loop — at massive speed and scale.

1.2.1 Data: The Fuel of All AI

Every AI system runs on data. Without quality data, even the most powerful AI is useless. Types of data AI works with include: Text & Numbers, Images & Video, Audio, and Sensor Data (GPS, IoT).

⚠️ The GIGO Principle
Garbage In, Garbage Out. If you feed an AI system poor-quality, biased, or irrelevant data — it will produce poor-quality, biased, or irrelevant results. Always ask: Who collected this data? Where did it come from? What biases might be hiding in it?

1.2.2 Machine Learning: How AI Actually Learns

Machine Learning is the engine inside most modern AI — the process by which computers learn to recognize patterns from data without being explicitly programmed for every situation.

TypeHow It WorksReal-World Example
Supervised LearningAI learns from labeled examplesSpam filters, credit scoring, medical diagnosis
Unsupervised LearningAI finds patterns in unlabeled dataCustomer segmentation, anomaly detection
Reinforcement LearningAI learns by trial and rewardGame-playing AI, robotics, self-driving cars

1.2.3 Three Types of AI Systems

🎯 Narrow AI (Weak AI)
Designed for one specific task. Examples: Siri, ChatGPT, Netflix recommendations, Claude, Gemini, Grok. This is what you will use every day.
🧬 AGI (Strong AI)
A theoretical AI that can learn and reason across ANY domain like a human being. Researchers are working toward it — it does not exist yet.
🚀 ASI (Superintelligence)
A hypothetical AI that would surpass human intelligence in every area. Purely theoretical — but critical for ethical discussions.

1.3 Key Concepts You Must Know

TermPlain-Language Definition
Generative AIAI that creates new content — text, images, music, video, code. Examples: ChatGPT, DALL-E, Suno, ElevenLabs.
LLMLarge Language Model — AI trained on billions of words of text. It predicts the next most likely word. The engine inside ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini.
HallucinationWhen AI generates false information and presents it as fact. Always verify important AI outputs with reliable sources.
Context WindowHow much text an AI can hold in its 'working memory'. Ranges from 4K to 128K+ tokens. The longer the window, the better the context.

PromptThe instruction or question you give to an AI system. Your prompt quality directly determines your output quality.
GANGenerative Adversarial Network — two AI systems competing. One creates content, the other critiques it until the output is convincing.

No single ingredient makes a great AI system. All four must work together.

📅 Week 1 · Lesson 2
Prompt Engineering Mastery
⏱️ ~40 minutes
🧠 The Most Critical Skill

The Art and Science of Communicating with AI

If data is the fuel of AI, then your prompt is the key that starts the engine. Prompt Engineering is the most important practical skill you will develop in this course. The difference between a mediocre AI output and an extraordinary one is almost always the quality of the prompt.

💬 Team21 Principle
"A vague question gets a vague answer. A precise, contextual prompt gets a result that changes your life." — Team21 Academy

2.1 Understanding Context First

Before you craft a single prompt, understand two critical technical facts:

  • Context Window: The amount of text an AI can 'hold in mind' during a conversation. Older models: ~4,000 tokens. Latest models: 128,000+ tokens. The more context you provide, the better the output.
  • Multi-Modal Input: Modern AI can process text, images, audio, and video simultaneously. A photo of your business can give AI more information than a paragraph of description.

2.2 The Three Fundamental Prompting Strategies

StrategyHow It WorksExample
Zero-ShotGive AI a task with no examples. Best for simple, unambiguous tasks.'Translate this sentence to French: I am happy to meet you.'
One-ShotProvide ONE example before asking for the task. Helps AI match your format.Provide a sample letter, then ask for a similar one to the Minister of Education.
Few-ShotGive MULTIPLE examples to establish a clear pattern. Best for complex outputs.Provide 3 sample social media posts, then ask for 5 more in the same style.

2.3 The CLEAR Framework — P.T.F.D.T.

The most powerful prompt structure for consistent, professional results. Use this framework for every important AI task.

P — PERSONA
Define who the AI should act as. 'You are an experienced financial consultant with 15 years in Cameroonian markets...' A strong persona dramatically improves output quality.
T — TONE
Specify the communication style. Options: Professional, casual, enthusiastic, empathetic, authoritative, bilingual (English/French), etc.
F — FORMAT
Describe exactly how you want the output structured. Options: bullet points, numbered list, table, essay, 8-slide presentation, 500-word report.
D — DETAILS
Provide specific requirements, background information, constraints, and context. The more relevant detail you give, the more precise and useful the output.
T — TARGET AUDIENCE
Identify who the output is for. 'This is for Cameroonian primary school children aged 8-12 who speak French.' This shapes vocabulary, complexity, and cultural references.

Full CLEAR Framework Example

PERSONA: You are an experienced (10 years) digital business and e-commerce expert specialising in emerging African markets.
TONE: Energetic, practical, and encouraging.
FORMAT: Weekly content teasers — 4 posts, one for each week. Each post should be 3-4 sentences maximum.
DETAILS: I am launching a new course teaching AI skills to entrepreneurs in Cameroon. The course costs 50,000 CFA and runs for 4 weeks online.
TARGET: Gen Z professionals aged 18-28 in Cameroon (francophone), who use WhatsApp and TikTok daily, and want to improve their income.

2.4 Multi-Modal Prompting

Input TypeExample Prompt
Text'Based on Cameroon's current challenges, who would be the best presidential candidate and why?'
Image[Upload a photo of a room] 'Help me redesign this space to be more professional. Include furniture, colours, and layout ideas.'
Voice'Give me 10 ideas on how to invest 500,000 CFA wisely in the current Cameroonian market.'
Document[Upload a PDF contract] 'Review this contract and flag any clauses unfavourable to the seller. Summarise in plain English and French.'
Role-Play'Simulate a panel of 3 interviewers for an AI trainer position. Ask me difficult questions and evaluate my answers.'

2.5 Common Prompting Mistakes to Avoid

  • Being too vague: 'Write me something about business' → Instead: 'Write a 300-word description of my poultry business for a LinkedIn post targeting investors in Cameroon.'
  • Skipping the persona: Without telling AI who it is, you get a generic response. Always assign a role.
  • Ignoring iteration: If the first output is not perfect, refine your prompt. Add more detail, change the tone, or provide examples.
  • Not specifying length: Always indicate how long you want the output to be.
  • Forgetting the audience: Who will read this? That changes everything about vocabulary, tone, and content.
🔑 Advanced Prompting Elements
CONTEXT — Background that helps AI understand your situation. REFERENCE — Examples or standards to follow. ITERATE — Your first prompt is rarely your best. EVALUATE — Always assess: Is it accurate? Is it appropriate?

3.3 Writing a Book with AI — The 8-Step Process

  1. VISION & AUDIENCE: Define your book's core purpose. What transformation do you want to create in your reader? Who exactly are they?
  2. TITLE BRAINSTORM: Ask AI to generate 15-20 possible titles reflecting your core idea, the problem you solve, and the reader's desired outcome.
  3. CHAPTER OUTLINE: Ask AI to develop 7 core chapters with clear learning objectives and a logical narrative flow. Review and refine.
  4. CONTENT DRAFTING: Write each chapter section by section — but always edit and add your own voice, examples, and local context.
  5. CHARACTERS & STORIES: Use AI to develop relatable characters and case studies grounded in your cultural context.
  6. VISUAL ELEMENTS: Generate illustrations, tables, and figures using AI image tools. Use Gamma for visual slides or frameworks.
  7. FRONT & BACK MATTER: Generate AI-assisted cover concept (InsMind/Ideogram), author bio, acknowledgements, and back cover copy.
  8. AUDIOBOOK: Use ElevenLabs to convert your book to audio — create a multilingual version to expand your reach.

3.4 Professional Communication at Scale

AI makes professional communication in multiple languages easy and fast:

  • Grant applications: Research granting organisations using Perplexity, then draft a complete application package.
  • Formal letters: AI can draft professional letters to ministers, embassies, and international organisations in English, French, Spanish, and Arabic.
  • Social media content: Generate platform-specific posts in multiple languages adapted for each audience.
  • Legal documents: Draft articles of association, contracts, and compliance documents (always have a lawyer review).

3.5 Ethical Use of AI-Generated Content

⚠️ Three Non-Negotiable Principles

1. DISCLOSE AI ASSISTANCE: If you use AI to generate content you publish under your name without significant editing, disclose it. Integrity matters more than appearing impressive.

2. VERIFY BEFORE PUBLISHING: AI can 'hallucinate' — making up facts, citations, and statistics. Every AI output shared publicly must be fact-checked.

3. OWN YOUR CREATIVE VOICE: AI should assist your creativity, not replace it. Always add your own perspective, local examples, and cultural context.

📅 Week 2 · Lesson 2
AI Entrepreneurship — BMC, SOSTAC & Social Media
⏱️ ~40 minutes
💼 Business Strategy

4.1 The Four Foundations of a Business

1. Identify a Real Problem
What specific issue are you solving? For whom? The best businesses are built around genuine pain — something people desperately need fixed.
2. Start with Your Own Capital
Begin with what you can afford to lose. Validate your concept with minimal investment before scaling. Avoid loans for unproven ideas.
3. Find a Mentor
Find someone who has already made the mistakes you will inevitably make. If necessary, pay them. A mentor saves you years of painful learning.
4. Build a Plan
Use the Business Model Canvas (BMC) to visualise your entire business model before you spend a single CFA franc on execution.

4.2 The Business Model Canvas (BMC)

The BMC is the most important one-page strategic tool in entrepreneurship. It forces you to answer the nine fundamental questions every business must answer:

#BlockKey Question
1Customer SegmentsWho are you serving?
2Value PropositionWhat problem do you solve?
3ChannelsHow do you reach them?
4Customer RelationshipsHow do you keep them?
5Revenue StreamsHow do you make money?
6Key ResourcesWhat do you need?
7Key ActivitiesWhat do you do?
8Key PartnersWho helps you?
9Cost StructureWhat does it cost to operate?
ROI = (Expected Revenue − Total Investment) ÷ Total Investment × 100
Example: You invest 100,000 CFA and earn 170,000 CFA → ROI = 70%. Aim for minimum 10% ROI.

4.3 The SOSTAC Framework

S — SITUATION
Where are you now? Analyse your market, identify problems, map your competitors.
O — OBJECTIVES
Where do you want to be? Set SMART goals — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
S — STRATEGY
How will you get there? Define your core offer, target customer, and key competitive advantage.
T — TACTICS
What specific actions will you take? Choose 2-3 platforms and execute with precision.
A — ACTION
Execute systematically. Create a daily action plan. Day 1: launch flyer. Day 3: direct-message 10 prospects.
C — CONTROL
How will you measure success? Track daily: content posted, responses received, revenue earned.

5.1 What Is Agentic AI?

Agentic AI refers to AI systems that can take autonomous, multi-step actions to complete complex tasks — not just respond to a single prompt.

Standard AIAgentic AI
You ask a question → AI gives one answerYou give a goal → AI plans steps, executes each one, evaluates results, adjusts, and continues until the goal is achieved

Real-World Examples of Agentic AI

  • Business Research Agent: You provide a business idea. The agent researches the market, analyses competition, identifies target customers, evaluates financial viability, and produces a comprehensive report.
  • Quiz Master Agent: Creates educational content, tests understanding, adapts difficulty based on student performance, and generates progress reports.
  • Location Analysis Agent: Evaluates multiple criteria (cost of living, job opportunities, infrastructure, safety, schools) to recommend optimal locations.
  • Personal Coaching Agent: Asks questions one at a time, builds a profile of your strengths and goals, and generates a customised Personal Growth Plan.

5.4 Programming with AI: Your First HTML Game

You do not need to be a developer to use AI to build software:

  1. DESCRIBE what you want in plain English: 'Create a simple quiz game about Cameroonian geography that asks 10 questions, keeps score, and shows results at the end.'
  2. SPECIFY the technology: 'Use HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. It should work in a browser without any installation.'
  3. ITERATE: Ask AI to fix bugs, add features, or improve the design. 'Add a timer countdown for each question.' 'Make it bilingual (English/French).'
  4. TEST everything. Paste the code into a text file, save as .html, and open in your browser.

6.3 The 3Ps Personal Growth Framework

P — PURPOSE
Why do you exist? What is the unique contribution you are called to make? Your purpose is the compass that guides every decision.
P — PLAN
A purpose without a plan is just a wish. Develop clear, measurable goals with timelines and accountability checkpoints.
P — PERFORMANCE
Execute your plan consistently. Track your results. Learn from failures. Celebrate progress. Performance compounds over time.

6.5 Servant Leadership — The Team21 Foundation

Servant leadership is the cornerstone of Team21 Academy's leadership philosophy. A servant leader:

  • Puts the growth and wellbeing of the team first
  • Listens deeply before speaking or deciding
  • Builds others' capabilities — shares knowledge, creates opportunities
  • Leads with integrity — does what they say, says what they do
  • Uses AI as a tool to serve more people better — not to cut corners or replace human connection
✅ AI + Leadership Integration

7.1 What Is the Capstone Project?

The Capstone Project is your opportunity to demonstrate everything you have learned over the past four weeks. It is not a test — it is a showcase. You will use AI to solve a real problem, build a real product, or develop a real business — and present it professionally.

7.2 Capstone Project Options — Choose One

Option A: AI-Powered Business
Create a complete business plan for a real venture: BMC, SOSTAC plan, marketing materials, financial projections, website/app prototype.
Option B: AI-Generated Book
Write and produce a complete bilingual book (PDF) with AI-generated visuals, proper formatting, and an audiobook version via ElevenLabs.
Option C: Community Solution
Use AI to design a solution to a real problem in your community — NGO programme, educational tool, health campaign, or infrastructure plan.
Option D: AI App/Tool
Build a working HTML/web-based tool or game using AI code generation. Must solve a real problem or provide real value.

7.3 Presentation Structure (15 Minutes)

  1. Introduction (2 min): Who you are, what problem you identified, and why it matters.
  2. Solution Overview (4 min): Present your AI-powered solution — what it is, who it serves, how it works.
  3. Live Demonstration (5 min): Show your product, app, business plan, or book. Live demo preferred.
  4. Impact & Next Steps (3 min): What impact will this have? What are your next 30 days? What resources do you need?
  5. Q&A (1 min): Open to questions from your mentor and peers.
