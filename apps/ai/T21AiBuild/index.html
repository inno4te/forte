1
From Reactive to Proactive AI Use
The Operator Mindset — The Fundamental Shift
▾

In Level 1 you learned to use AI. You asked questions and got answers. That was the beginning. Level 1 was about access — getting into the building. Level 2 is about operating the building.

Most people who use AI are in a reactive relationship with it. They have a problem, they ask AI for help, they copy the output. This is useful but limited. An AI operator has a fundamentally different relationship with the technology.

Operators design systems in advance. They build libraries of prompts that are ready the moment they need them. They chain tools together so that one action triggers ten outcomes. They use AI to think, not just to produce.

AI UserAI Operator
Asks questions one at a timeDesigns prompt systems in advance

Uses one tool at a timeChains tools together for compound results

"The difference between a user and an operator is this: a user asks AI what to do. An operator tells AI what to build." — Innocent Forteh, Team21 Academy

Advanced Prompting: AI as a Thinking Partner
Four techniques that transform AI from tool to intellectual partner
📖 22 min🧪 4 techniques to practice
1
Technique 1: Scenario Modelling
Map multiple futures from a single decision
▾

Scenario modelling means feeding a real decision you are facing into AI and asking it to map out multiple possible futures. Instead of asking "what should I do?", you ask: "if I do X, walk me through the best-case, worst-case, and most-likely outcomes over the next 90 days, including risks I may not have considered."

💬 Sample Prompt — Scenario Modelling
You are a senior strategic adviser with 20 years of experience in small business development in Central Africa. I am considering [describe your situation]. Map out three scenarios — optimistic, realistic, and pessimistic — for the next 90 days if I proceed. For each scenario, identify the top three risks and one specific action I should take in the first 7 days. Be direct and specific.
2
Technique 2: Critical Advocate Prompting
Find every flaw before you commit
▾

One of the most powerful uses of AI is to have it actively argue against you. This is not comfortable, but it is enormously valuable. Before you commit to a business decision, a proposal, or a plan, ask AI to find every flaw.

💬 Sample Prompt — Critical Advocate
I am about to [describe your plan or decision]. Before I proceed, I want you to act as the most intelligent, well-informed critic of this idea. Identify every significant weakness, hidden risk, false assumption, and alternative I have not considered. Do not soften your critique. Be comprehensive and ruthless. Then, once you have finished, suggest the top three ways I could strengthen this plan given what you have identified.
3
Technique 3: Iterative Refinement Chains
Build conversations that get progressively better
▾

Rather than asking for a final output in one prompt, build a conversation that gets progressively better. Start broad, then refine. This technique is especially powerful for complex documents, strategies, and content.

🔧 How To Do It — Step by Step
1
Give Claude the context and ask for a first draft or outline.
2
Review the output. Identify what is right, what is missing, and what tone feels wrong.
3
Feed specific observations back: "The structure is good but the tone is too formal. Paragraph 3 misses the point — the key issue is X. Revise with these changes."
4
Repeat until the output meets your standard. Three to five iterations typically produces something excellent.
4
Technique 4: Role Stacking
An interdisciplinary team in one response
▾

Role stacking means assigning Claude a composite identity that draws on multiple expert perspectives at once. Rather than a single expert, you get an interdisciplinary team in one response.

💬 Sample Prompt — Role Stacking
You will combine the analytical perspective of a McKinsey consultant, the communication instincts of an experienced journalist, and the practical wisdom of a Cameroonian entrepreneur who has built businesses with limited resources. From this combined perspective, review the following [plan/document/idea] and give me your assessment, your top three recommendations, and the single most important thing I am currently overlooking.
💡 Trainer Tip
Do these four techniques as live demonstrations. For each one, have students contribute actual content — use a real student's business idea for the Critical Advocate exercise. The emotional impact of seeing their own plan critiqued in real time is unforgettable.

1
What is Chain-of-Thought & Why Does It Work?
Research-backed technique from AI labs
▾

Chain-of-thought prompting is a technique developed by AI researchers that dramatically improves the quality of reasoning in large language models. The principle is simple: instead of asking for an answer directly, you ask the AI to think out loud — to show its work step by step before giving a conclusion.

💬 Sample Prompt — Chain-of-Thought
Before you give me your final answer, I want you to think through this problem step by step. Show me your reasoning at each stage. Consider what information you have, what assumptions you are making, where there is uncertainty, and what the logic is before you conclude. Only then give me your recommendation. Question: [your question here]

Research has consistently shown that chain-of-thought prompting produces more accurate, more nuanced, and more actionable responses — particularly for complex, multi-part problems. When the stakes matter, always prompt for chain-of-thought reasoning.

Q3: What is chain-of-thought prompting and why does it improve AI output quality? Q4: What does 'role stacking' mean? Q5: How many iterations typically produces an excellent result in refinement chains?

What is Storyboarding & Why It Matters
The visual plan that makes every AI output better
▾

A storyboard is a visual plan — a sequence of frames mapping out what the viewer sees and hears, in what order, and why. Originally used in film, storyboarding is now the most powerful planning tool for AI content creators.

"AI can generate anything you describe. The quality of your storyboard is the quality of your output." — Team21 Academy

The 5 Elements of Every Storyboard Frame

ElementWhat to Define
Scene NumberSequential order of this frame
Visual DescriptionWhat the viewer sees — setting, characters, action
Audio / DialogueVoiceover, music, sound effects, speech
DurationHow long this frame lasts (in seconds)
TransitionHow this frame moves to the next — cut, fade, wipe

Creating Your Storyboard with Claude

▾
💬 Storyboard Generation Prompt
You are an experienced storyboard artist. Create a detailed storyboard for a [duration] video about [topic]. Audience: [describe]. Platform: [YouTube/Reel/TikTok]. Core message: [takeaway]. Tone: [e.g., inspirational]. Format each frame as: FRAME [N] | VISUAL: [what is seen] | AUDIO: [voiceover/music/sound] | DURATION: [seconds] | TRANSITION: [cut/fade/zoom]. Include [X] frames total. Make each frame specific enough that an AI image or video tool could execute it from description alone.
